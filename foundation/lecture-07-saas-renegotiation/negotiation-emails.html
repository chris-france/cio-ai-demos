To:Account Executive, Snowflake CC:CFO, VP Engineering Subject:Contract Review — Usage Optimization & Renewal Terms for [Company]

Dear [Snowflake AE Name],

As we approach our January 2026 renewal, I'd like to open a conversation about optimizing our Snowflake contract to better align with our actual usage patterns.

Our internal audit has surfaced several data points I'd like to discuss:

Current annual contract$240,000
Licensed users20
Active users18
Effective cost per user/month$1,000
Comparable alternatives evaluatedClickHouse, DuckDB, Apache Spark

At $1,000 per user per month, Snowflake is our single most expensive SaaS tool on a per-seat basis. While we value the platform's performance and ease of use, this cost level requires us to evaluate whether the current contract structure is sustainable for our organization.

We've begun preliminary evaluation of open-source alternatives including ClickHouse for our analytical workloads. We'd prefer to continue our partnership with Snowflake, but we need the economics to work. Specifically, we're looking for:

1. A minimum 25-30% reduction in our annual commitment
2. A shift to a consumption-based model with committed-use discounts rather than flat licensing
3. Quarterly usage reviews with right-sizing recommendations from your team

I'd appreciate the opportunity to discuss this before our renewal window. Could we schedule a call in the next two weeks?

[Your Name]
Chief Information Officer
[Company]
Negotiation Strategy Notes (do not send): Snowflake's biggest fear is losing you to Databricks or open-source. Mentioning ClickHouse signals you've done homework. Ask for consumption-based pricing — Snowflake's own model supports this, and it usually saves 20-40% for mid-size customers. Don't threaten to leave; let the data speak. The $1,000/user/month number is shocking enough on its own.
$933/USER/MO $168,000/yr Overlaps with Datadog
To:Account Executive, Splunk CC:CFO, CISO Subject:Splunk Cloud Renewal — Consolidation Discussion for [Company]

Dear [Splunk AE Name],

I'm writing regarding our Splunk Cloud contract, which is scheduled for renewal on December 31, 2025. As part of a broader SaaS rationalization initiative, we've completed an audit of our monitoring and observability stack that I'd like to share with you.

Current Splunk annual cost$168,000
Licensed users15
Active users12
Effective cost per user/month$933
Overlapping toolDatadog Pro ($115,200/yr)
Combined monitoring spend$283,200/yr

Our engineering team currently uses both Splunk and Datadog, which creates significant overlap in log analysis, dashboarding, and alerting capabilities. We are consolidating to a single observability platform, and we need to make a decision before your renewal date.

Candidly, Datadog currently handles the majority of our real-time monitoring workflows, and our team has expressed a preference for its interface. However, Splunk's SIEM capabilities and compliance reporting are features Datadog doesn't fully replicate.

If we are to continue with Splunk, we would need:

1. A 40% reduction in annual cost to reflect our actual 12-user footprint
2. A right-sized contract at our actual ingest volume, not the peak capacity we're currently paying for
3. Inclusion of SIEM/compliance dashboards at no additional cost — this is the only feature keeping Splunk in our stack

Without a significantly restructured agreement, we will consolidate fully to Datadog at renewal. I'd welcome a conversation about how we can make this work for both sides.

[Your Name]
Chief Information Officer
[Company]
Negotiation Strategy Notes (do not send): This is your strongest hand. You have a working alternative (Datadog) already deployed. Splunk knows their biggest churn risk is Datadog consolidation. Lead with the overlap data, not emotion. The 40% ask is aggressive but justified — you're paying for 15 seats and using 12, at nearly $1K/user/month. Splunk will likely counter at 20-25%. Your walk-away is real: you can actually cancel. That's leverage.
$200/USER/MO $120,000/yr 44% Seats Unused
To:Account Executive, Coupa CC:CFO, VP Finance Subject:Coupa Procurement — License Right-Sizing Before September Renewal

Dear [Coupa AE Name],

Our Coupa Procurement contract is coming up for renewal on September 30, 2025, and I'd like to discuss right-sizing our agreement before auto-renewal takes effect.

Current annual cost$120,000
Licensed users50
Active users28
Utilization rate56%
Unused licenses22 seats ($52,800/yr wasted)
Cost per active user/month$357

We are currently paying for 50 licenses but only 28 people actively use the platform. That means 44% of our Coupa investment — approximately $52,800 per year — is going to unused seats. At $200 per user per month on a per-license basis (or $357 per active user), this is well above market rate for procurement software.

We've evaluated several alternatives including open-source options (ERPNext procurement module) and mid-market competitors (Precoro, Kissflow Procurement Cloud) that offer comparable P2P workflows at 40-60% lower cost per seat.

For our renewal, we're requesting:

1. A contract restructured for 30 seats (our actual usage plus a small buffer), not 50
2. A per-seat rate reduction of at least 15% given our multi-year tenure and the current market
3. Elimination of auto-renewal — we prefer annual opt-in renewals going forward

This would bring our annual cost to approximately $61,200 — a fair price for the value delivered. I'd like to resolve this before the September renewal window. Can we schedule a call this week?

[Your Name]
Chief Information Officer
[Company]
Negotiation Strategy Notes (do not send): Coupa's weakness here is the utilization data — 56% is indefensible. They'll try to argue "you might grow into it." Counter with: "We've had 50 seats for the full contract term and never exceeded 28 active users." The auto-renewal elimination ask is important — it forces them to earn your business every year. Mention Precoro by name; it's a real competitor they track. Target outcome: $61K (49% reduction). You'll likely land around $72-78K (35-38% savings). That's still $42-48K saved.
